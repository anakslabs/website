Where it goes wrong

It looks finished. It can still be unreadable.

None of this shows up when you look at your own website. It shows up when something has to read that page and answer a question with it.

  • Your most important sentence is a picture

    Your best line is set into the page as an image, so the type looks exactly right. A person reads it. Google receives an empty box where that sentence should be, and nothing can quote a sentence it was never given.

  • Text inside a slider

    The panels that change on their own. Usually they are not in the page when it arrives — a script adds them a moment later. What a visitor swipes through, Google and the assistants are often never handed at all.

  • Answers locked in a PDF

    Prices, details and instructions posted as downloads. A person can open it. An assistant is far less likely to quote it.

  • Nothing tells a machine what you actually do

    No questions answered in the format search engines recognise, no page for each thing you offer, no opening hours anywhere a machine can pick them up. Organised to a person, one undifferentiated block of text to a machine.

  • Nothing you can measure

    A phone number and a contact form, and no way to tell which visits turned into customers.
